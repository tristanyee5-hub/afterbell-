        Study guide

        Know this first

        Four short guides. All of it is free, and all of it matters more than anything in your cart.

        • Guide 1 · Fuel

          Food first

          • Three meals and a snack or two cover what most young athletes need. Shakes and bars fill gaps. They don't replace meals.
          • Get protein at every meal: eggs, chicken, milk, yogurt, beans or peanut butter.
          • Eat within an hour or two after training. A sandwich works. A shake or a bar is for when real food is hours away.
          • Extra protein doesn't turn into extra muscle. Training, sleep and enough total food do that.
          See the fuel picks

        • Guide 2 · Hydration

          Drink early

          • Show up hydrated. Drink water with every meal and keep a bottle with you through the day.
          • At practice, take a few big gulps every 15 to 20 minutes. Don't wait until you're thirsty.
          • Water is enough for most practices. Past an hour of hard work, or in real heat, a sports drink or electrolyte mix helps replace what you sweat out.
          • Check the color. Pale yellow means you're good. Dark yellow means drink more.
          See the hydration picks

        • Guide 3 · Training

          Form before weight

          • Learn each lift with light weight first. Ask a coach or athletic trainer to watch your squat, deadlift, press and row.
          • Lift two or three days a week, with a rest day in between.
          • Add weight in small jumps, and only when every rep looks the same.
          • No weight room? Push-ups, pull-ups, band rows, squats and a jump rope train the whole body.
          See the gear picks

        • Guide 4 · Recovery

          Sleep is the supplement

          • Aim for 8 to 10 hours a night. That's the recommendation for teenagers, and it's when your body rebuilds.
          • Roll each tight muscle slowly for 30 to 60 seconds. It should feel like pressure, not pain.
          • The day after a hard session, move easy: walk, jog light or shoot around.
          • Soreness that fades in a day or two is normal. Sharp pain, swelling or pain that sticks around isn't. Tell a coach, trainer or parent.
          See the recovery picks

        General tips, not medical advice. Your coach, athletic trainer, parents and doctor know you, so their advice comes first.

        Questions

        Do I actually need supplements?

        Mostly no. Regular meals cover what a high school athlete needs. Powder, shakes and bars are food in a form that survives a backpack, which helps when practice ends hours after lunch.

        Is protein powder OK for teenagers?

        Protein powder is food, and most teens who eat regular meals already get enough protein. Treat it as a convenience, never a requirement. If you have allergies or a medical condition, or you're unsure, ask a parent and your doctor first, and read the label.

        Why no pre-workout?

        This store is built for high schoolers, so it sticks to food basics and gear. Pre-workout, fat burners and test boosters are made for adults. If you're curious about anything stronger, talk to a parent, your doctor or your athletic trainer.
